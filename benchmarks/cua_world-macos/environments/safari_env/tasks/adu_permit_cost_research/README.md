# Task: ADU Permit & Cost Research (San Jose, CA)

## Scenario

You own a single-family home in Willow Glen, San Jose, CA (ZIP 95125) and need to build a **detached Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU)** for your mother, who uses a wheelchair. Target size: **400–450 square feet**. The ADU must meet ADA-accessible design standards. You want to understand the permitting requirements, realistic total costs, available local contractors, and financing options before committing.

Research and compile your findings. **Save your research as a new note in Apple Notes** so you can share it with your spouse and contractor before making any decisions.

## What to Include in the Note

The note should be detailed enough to walk into a permitting office and contractor meeting prepared. Cover at minimum:
- San Jose ADU permit requirements: setbacks, maximum size, height limits, owner-occupancy rule, estimated permit fees
- ADA/Title 24 accessibility requirements for residential ADUs (doorway widths, turning radius, roll-in shower, etc.)
- At least 3 cost estimates by ADU type (site-built, prefab/modular, JADU), with cost ranges per sq ft
- At least 3 local San Jose–area contractors with ADU experience and accessibility/ADA notes
- At least 2 financing options (CalHFA ADU Grant, HELOC, ADU construction loan, etc.) with current amounts/rates
- Source citations (city ADU page, state law, pricing guides, contractor sites)

## Difficulty

**very_hard** — requires:
- Finding San Jose's *current* ADU ordinance specifics (setbacks, height, owner-occupancy rule — changed post-2020 statewide preemption)
- Distinguishing city-level rules from California statewide ADU law (AB 68, SB 897, etc.)
- Finding cost estimates for detached ADUs at 400–450 sq ft — data is scarce and varies dramatically by build type (site-built vs prefab vs JADU)
- Locating San Jose–area contractors who specifically list ADU + accessibility experience (not generic GCs)
- Researching ADU-specific financing (CalHFA ADU Grant, HELOCs, ADU construction loans) with current 2024/2025 rates/limits
- Understanding ADA/Title 24 accessibility requirements in residential ADU context

## Hardness Levers

1. Multi-source synthesis: city permit portal + state ADU guides + contractor directories + financing programs
2. Geography constraint: San Jose rules specifically (not generic California ADU)
3. Personal constraint: wheelchair accessibility introduces additional code requirements
4. Conflicting information: statewide law vs city ordinance; costs vary widely by source
5. Real-world specificity: actual CSLB-licensed contractors near 95125; actual state/city grant programs
6. Output depth: must cover permits, costs, contractors, AND financing in a single organized note
