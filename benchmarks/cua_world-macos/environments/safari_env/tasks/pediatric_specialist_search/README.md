# Task: Pediatric Rheumatologist Research for JIA

## Scenario

Your 9-year-old was diagnosed with **juvenile idiopathic arthritis (polyarticular RF-negative JIA)** three weeks ago by your general pediatrician in Naperville, Illinois. The pediatrician has referred you to a specialist but given you no specific names. You need to find a pediatric rheumatologist who:
- Is affiliated with an academic medical center (for access to clinical trials and the latest biologics)
- Accepts **Blue Cross Blue Shield PPO** insurance
- Is located within **60 miles of Naperville, IL** (zip 60540)

You also need to understand the treatment landscape — what options exist, and what questions to bring to the first appointment.

Research pediatric rheumatologists, JIA treatment guidelines, and disease-management resources. **Save your findings as a new note in Apple Notes** so you can refer to it when calling to make appointments and when you're in the office.

## What to Include in the Note

The note should be detailed enough to be genuinely useful at the appointment. Cover at minimum:
- At least 3 specific doctors (name, hospital, city, how to contact) with notes on whether they accept BCBS and have academic affiliation
- Your understanding of the treatment options available for polyarticular RF-negative JIA — what the main drugs are, what class they belong to, what they're used for
- At least 6 specific, substantive questions you plan to ask at the appointment (not generic "what are the side effects" — real questions about biologics, clinical trials, school accommodations, etc.)

You decide how to organize the note — it's your personal research document.

## Difficulty

**very_hard** — requires:
- Finding real pediatric rheumatologists (not adult rheumatologists) within the geographic constraint
- Cross-referencing hospital affiliation against insurance directories (BCBS)
- Understanding a specialized pediatric condition to formulate informed appointment questions
- Synthesizing from medical directories, hospital sites, insurance tools, and disease-focused organizations

## Hardness Levers

1. Multi-source synthesis: hospital directories + insurance verifier + specialty org + clinical trial registry
2. Multi-stage decisions: diagnosis → specialist search → treatment understanding → appointment prep
3. Family medical: a parent researching for their sick child, not a professional
4. Conflicting information: different sources may list different accepted insurances
5. Real-world specificity: actual doctors, hospitals, phone numbers, drug classes

## Verification Strategy

1. A fresh Apple Notes note exists (created/modified after task start)
2. A medical directory or children's hospital site was visited (healthgrades, zocdoc, luriechildrens.org, nm.org, uchicagomedicine.org, etc.)
3. A disease/treatment reference site was visited (arthritis.org, rheumatology.org, creakyjoints.org, clinicaltrials.gov, etc.)
4. Note has substantial content length
5. Note contains relevant domain keywords (rheumatologist, specialist, hospital, bcbs, treatment, appointment, arthritis, etc.)
6. Note has comprehensive length and keyword coverage for full credit

**Pass threshold**: 70/100
