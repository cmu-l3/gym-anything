# Task: Used Electric Vehicle Purchase Research

## Scenario

You live in Austin, TX and are replacing your 2015 Honda Civic. You want to buy a used electric vehicle with a max budget of $28,000 out-the-door. You have two kids (car seats required) and a 40-mile round-trip daily commute. You rent an apartment with Level 1 (120V) outlet access only — no Level 2 charger installation allowed.

Research and compare **at least 3 specific used EV models** (2020–2023 model years) and **save your comparison as a new Apple Notes note**.

## What to Include in the Note

Cover at minimum:
- Each model's typical used price range and estimated OTD cost in Texas (sales tax ~6.25%)
- EPA-rated range and real-world range estimate for Austin's climate
- Level 1 charging rate (miles added per hour at 120V) and overnight charge time from 20% to 80%
- Cargo space / rear seat room (car seat LATCH ratings if available)
- Reliability and owner satisfaction data with source
- Federal used EV tax credit eligibility (IRA §25E) for each model
- Nearest Austin dealer or certified pre-owned program for each brand
- Total estimated 3-year ownership cost vs your 2015 Civic

## Difficulty

**very_hard** — requires:
- Matching EV range reality to Level 1-only charging constraint (rules out many long-range EVs as impractical)
- Finding used EV pricing at Austin-area specific inventory (not national averages)
- Calculating Level 1 charge rates per model — these vary by onboard charger amp rating (16A vs 32A → still limited to ~1.4 kW on L1)
- Researching IRS §25E used EV credit eligibility (price cap $25K, income limits, MSRP restriction for specific models)
- Comparing car-seat suitability and cargo space for family with two young children
- Multi-source synthesis: EV specs (EPA, PlugStar) + pricing (CarGurus, Autotrader) + reliability (Consumer Reports, JD Power) + tax rules (IRS)

## Hardness Levers

1. Budget constraint: $28K OTD with Texas sales tax math
2. Infrastructure constraint: Level 1 only → impacts model viability
3. Family constraint: car seats, cargo
4. Financial complexity: used EV tax credit has strict eligibility rules per model/price
5. Real-world specificity: Austin dealer inventory, not just national listings
6. Multi-source synthesis across EV specs, pricing, reliability, and tax guidance

## Verification Strategy

1. A fresh Apple Notes note exists (created/modified after task start)
2. A vehicle pricing/marketplace site was visited (cargurus.com, autotrader.com, cars.com, carmax.com, kbb.com)
3. An EV information or tax/financial site was visited (fueleconomy.gov, plugstar.com, irs.gov, edmunds.com, consumerreports.org)
4. Note has substantial content length
5. Note contains relevant domain keywords (range, charging, electric, tax, credit, battery, lease, reliability, commute)
6. Note is comprehensive (both length and keyword thresholds)

**Pass threshold**: 70/100
